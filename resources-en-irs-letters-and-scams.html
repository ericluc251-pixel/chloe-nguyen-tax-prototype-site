Got a letter from the IRS? How to tell if it is real

The IRS usually contacts you first by mail. Here is how to read a notice, and the signs of a scam.

By Chloe Nguyen Tax Advisory · Published August 25, 2026

Most IRS contact starts with a letter in the mail. A letter is not always bad news: it may ask you to confirm your identity or explain a change to your refund.

Read it calmly

  • Find the notice number, usually in the top or bottom right corner, such as CP2000 or 5071C
  • Check the tax year and the date you need to respond by
  • Compare it with your return before you pay or agree to anything

Signs of a scam

  • The IRS does not start contact by text message, email or social media
  • The IRS does not demand payment by gift card, wire transfer or cryptocurrency
  • The IRS does not threaten to have you arrested or deported
  • Do not call a phone number from a text or email; use the number on IRS.gov or in your IRS online account
